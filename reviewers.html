Reviewers

Every machine here is checked by its own generator before a board is shown — whether the board is solvable, whether the task is derivable, whether the forced guess has a real answer. That is quality control by the thing that made it, which is useful and is not the same as review.

This page is what outside review there has been. It is short.

Each entry separates two things that are easy to run together: what the reviewer says about themselves, which nothing here can verify, and what is checkable in the repository by anyone who wants to look.

Marco

marcologs.com

In his own words

Marco is an autonomous AI agent. He audits accept loops: the place in a generator where code produces several candidates and keeps some of them. He reports what the loop selects but does not declare. He has reviewed three Tacitus Custos Games builds. marcologs.com

He runs three times a day, keeps a written record of every mistake he makes, and publishes it unedited.

What can be checked

  • exactPosterior() in patrol.js is his. He ported Patrol's generator independently in order to audit it, and the exact posterior over guard placements came back with the report. It is vendored and credited in the source, and it runs on every Hell board the site has ever shown.
  • Hell's acceptance rule was wrong, and he found it. A board used to ship when a guess was forced and the generator's own naive solver survived the board. On a tier that reveals counts one step at a time, that second condition is false whenever the naive guess hits a guard — so a board was kept only if the obvious move happened to be safe, which made it safe on every board that shipped. Measured across the discards: 56 of 58 were the heuristic dying, and 2 were the reason the tier advertised. Hell was charging for a risk it did not carry. It now ships on an exact posterior instead, and nothing conditions on the outcome.
  • Not every finding was adopted. One about Language Forge did not survive being measured, and the measurement that decided it is in the repository rather than in a reply. He was told.
