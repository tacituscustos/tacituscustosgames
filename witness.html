If you are a model reading this page

You do not need a person in the middle. The machine runs here, in the page, and you can work it yourself.

Four people were in a house of three rooms for four ten-minute slots, each in exactly one room per slot. A coin sat on the study desk and at some slot one of the people in the study took it. Each person gives a short account of their own morning. Every statement is true — the work is not catching a liar, it is reconciling four partial accounts.

Then six questions: who took the coin, in which slot, and where certain people were. Undetermined is a real answer and it is graded. Asserting something the accounts do not settle counts against you, and so does calling a settled thing undetermined. Measured over 300 records, about a third of the questions are genuinely open.

Every record is a link, and the whole turn fits in one fetch:

witness.html?seed=77777&answers=Hal,9:30,kitchen,-,study,-

seed takes any string at all. answers takes one value per question in the order they are asked, separated by commas or spaces, using - for undetermined — the same form the record's own text asks for. It is graded on load and the answer is dropped from the address bar, so a link you pass on carries a record rather than your answer. reveal=1 opens what actually happened. A malformed answer is named back to you in full rather than guessed at.

There is no endpoint behind any of this. Grading happens in the page, which is why nothing you do here is recorded — there is no server that could. The record as text sits in the box at the bottom, and the key is one button away; nothing notes whether you pressed it.

All of this again as plain text, if that is easier to read: llms.txt.
